## English Original

### A Data Story Needs a Clear Sequence

Fictional home-service platform FixFox prepared a quarterly presentation with polished charts. One slide showed bookings, the next showed technician supply, and another showed cancellations. Each chart was understandable, but the audience could not see how the facts connected. The deck was a collection of observations rather than a story that supported a decision.

The analyst rebuilt the sequence around one message: growth was creating a weekend capacity problem in two cities. The context established that total bookings were 14% above plan. The change showed weekend demand rising 31% while weekday demand rose 6%. The explanation connected this pattern to a new advertising schedule, while clearly labeling the connection as supported but not experimentally proven.

Next came impact. Limited technician supply pushed weekend fulfillment from 91% to 78%, creating an estimated $220,000 quarterly revenue risk and more customer rescheduling. Finally, the recommendation proposed shifting recruitment incentives to the two affected cities and moving some advertising spend to weekdays. It named owners, a four-week test, success metrics, and a cost boundary.

Every chart now had a sentence title that stated its conclusion. Details that did not change the decision moved to an appendix. Consistent colors represented demand, capacity, and risk across slides. The analyst used one definition of weekend and one comparison period throughout. She also included an alternative explanation—seasonal events—and the evidence needed to assess it.

The final sequence was context, change, explanation, impact, and action. This order matched the questions listeners naturally asked. What situation are we in? What changed? What may have caused it? Why does it matter? What should happen next? Data storytelling is not decoration and should not force evidence into a dramatic narrative. Its job is to make the reasoning traceable. A reader should be able to disagree with an assumption without becoming lost in the structure.

## Chinese Translation

FixFox原汇报由互不连接的图表组成。分析师围绕“两个城市出现周末容量问题”重排：背景是预订高于计划14%，变化是周末需求增31%，解释与广告排期相关，影响是履约率降至78%并带来22万美元风险，最后提出招募和投放调整。

每张图用结论句作标题，非决策细节移到附录，并保留替代解释。清晰顺序让推理可追踪，而不是为了戏剧效果强行讲故事。

## Vocabulary

- **sequence** — 顺序；ordered flow of reasoning.
- **fulfillment** — 履约；successful completion of requested service.
- **appendix** — 附录；supporting detail outside the main flow.
- **traceable** — 可追踪的；easy to follow from evidence to conclusion.
- **alternative explanation** — 替代解释；another plausible cause.

## Grammar Analysis

- “a sentence title **that stated its conclusion**”中that限定标题功能。
- “without **becoming lost**”使用without加动名词表达避免的结果。

## Data Analyst Extension

汇报大纲先写五句：context、change、explanation、impact、action，再为每句选择最少证据。图表标题写结论，不写“Bookings by Week”这类主题名。

## Reading Questions

1. What was the central message? 2. What was the business impact? 3. Why was an alternative explanation included?

### Workplace Application

You have charts on traffic, conversion, and support tickets but no clear narrative. Write a 40–80 word proposed sequence leading to one decision.

### Answer Key

1. Growth caused weekend capacity risk in two cities. 2. Lower fulfillment and revenue risk. 3. To show uncertainty honestly. The application should connect context, change, explanation, impact, and action.
