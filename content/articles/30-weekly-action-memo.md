## English Original

### Write a Weekly Memo That Leads to Action

Fictional payment company ClearMint sent a weekly analytics email containing 18 metrics. Readers scanned the numbers but rarely replied. The memo reported that transactions were up, disputes were down, and onboarding was stable, yet it did not explain what mattered or who should act. It functioned as a data inventory rather than a management tool.

The analyst redesigned the memo with five sections. The first sentence gave the headline: transaction growth remained healthy, but a verification delay threatened next month's merchant activation. The evidence section listed only three numbers needed to support that conclusion. The driver section separated confirmed causes from open hypotheses. The risk section estimated affected merchants and timing. The action section named an owner, next step, and due date.

One update read: “New merchant activation fell 6% week over week because median document review time rose from 14 to 26 hours after a queue-routing change. About 420 applicants are currently delayed. Operations will restore the prior routing rule today and report recovery by Thursday.” A second bullet stated that weekend staffing might also contribute, but required another week of data.

Stable metrics moved to a compact appendix with targets and status markers. The main memo used consistent comparison periods and linked to definitions. Every claim had either a measured value or a confidence label. If no action was needed, the memo said “monitor” and named the condition that would trigger action. This prevented invented recommendations.

Responses improved because readers could quickly see the decision and responsibility. The memo did not become longer; it became more selective. A useful weekly update answers what changed, why it matters, what is known, what remains uncertain, and what happens next. ClearMint also reviewed previous actions at the top of the next memo, closing the loop between analysis and execution. Reporting created value when it changed attention or behavior, not when it merely repeated available data.

## Chinese Translation

ClearMint周报列18个指标，却没有重点与责任人。新版按headline、evidence、drivers、risk和action组织，只保留支持结论的数字，区分确认原因与假设，并写负责人、下一步和日期。

稳定指标移到附录；无需行动时明确“监控”及触发条件。下一周先回顾上次行动，形成分析到执行的闭环。周报价值来自改变注意力或行为，而不是重复数据。

## Vocabulary

- **data inventory** — 数据清单；a list without decision guidance.
- **threaten** — 威胁；create a meaningful future risk.
- **queue routing** — 队列路由；rules assigning work items.
- **trigger condition** — 触发条件；a rule that starts an action.
- **close the loop** — 闭环；review whether an action produced the result.

## Grammar Analysis

- “condition **that would trigger action**”用would描述在未来条件下的结果。
- “not when it merely repeated...”使用not when对比价值不产生的情况。

## Data Analyst Extension

周报模板固定为headline、3 key numbers、confirmed drivers、hypotheses、risk、action owner/date、previous action review。没有结论的指标放附录。

## Reading Questions

1. Why did the old memo fail?
2. What five sections replaced it?
3. How did the next memo close the loop?

### Workplace Application

Activation falls 5%, 300 users are delayed, and Operations can fix a routing rule by Friday. Write a 40–80 word weekly memo headline and action paragraph.

### Answer Key

1. The old memo lacked priority and action.
2. Headline, evidence, drivers, risk, and action replaced the metric list.
3. The next memo reviewed previous actions and their results.

### Application Model Response

Activation fell 5% this week after queue-routing delays affected about 300 users. The routing change is the largest confirmed driver; weekend staffing remains under review. Operations will restore the previous rule today, monitor the delayed queue, and report activation recovery by Friday. We will escalate if the backlog remains above 100 users tomorrow.
