## English Original

### Size an Opportunity with Transparent Assumptions

Fictional delivery platform EmberDrop considered a service for small towns. A proposal claimed a $50 million annual opportunity by multiplying the national population by average city spending. The number looked precise but assumed every resident was reachable, interested, and equally valuable.

The analyst built a driver model. She started with 1.2 million adults in towns the network could serve. Survey and comparable-market evidence suggested 35% might try delivery. Expected first-year activation was 18% of that group, monthly ordering was 1.6 orders, and contribution margin was $3.20 per order. This produced a much smaller base-case value.

Every input had a source, owner, confidence level, and range. The downside case used slower activation and lower frequency; the upside case assumed denser merchant coverage. The model separated total addressable demand from realistically serviceable and obtainable demand. It also included launch costs, courier density, and restaurant acquisition constraints.

Sensitivity analysis showed that order frequency and courier utilization mattered more than the population estimate. The recommendation was a three-town pilot designed to learn those two inputs. Success thresholds and a maximum loss were set before launch. The model would be updated with observed results rather than defended as a forecast.

Opportunity sizing is useful when it exposes the logic behind a decision. It is dangerous when a single large number hides uncertain multiplication. EmberDrop presented a range, named the assumptions most likely to change the result, and proposed an inexpensive way to learn. Transparent assumptions made the estimate easier to challenge and therefore more useful.

The model also showed a break-even threshold. If monthly frequency stayed below 1.2 orders, the service could not cover fixed operations even under optimistic adoption. Leaders used that threshold as a pilot guardrail instead of celebrating registrations that had little economic value.

The final document distinguished facts from assumptions visually and dated every external input. That made later revisions straightforward when merchant coverage changed.

## Chinese Translation
虚构配送平台EmberDrop正在考虑为小城镇推出服务。一份提案用全国人口乘以城市居民的平均消费，得出每年5000万美元的市场机会。这个数字看起来很精确，却默认每位居民都可以触达、愿意使用，而且价值完全相同。

分析师重新建立了一个驱动模型。她先计算配送网络能够覆盖的小城镇中的120万名成年人。调查和相似市场证据表明，其中35%可能尝试配送服务；第一年激活率预计为这一群体的18%；每名用户每月下单1.6次；每单贡献利润为3.20美元。这样算出的基准情景远小于原来的数字。

每项输入都记录来源、负责人、信心水平和变化范围。下行情景使用较慢的激活率和较低的下单频次；上行情景假设商户覆盖更加密集。模型把理论上可以触达的需求、现实中可以服务的需求以及实际可能获得的需求分开，还纳入启动成本、骑手密度和餐厅拓展限制。

敏感性分析显示，下单频次和骑手利用率对结果的影响大于人口估计。团队因此建议在三个城镇开展试点，专门学习这两个关键输入。上线前先设定成功阈值和最大可承受损失。模型会根据观察结果更新，而不是被当作必须维护的预测结论。

机会估算的价值在于暴露决策背后的逻辑。如果一个很大的数字隐藏着多项不确定乘法，它就会带来危险。EmberDrop提供区间，指出最可能改变结果的假设，并提出低成本的学习方式。透明假设让估算更容易被质疑，也因此更有用。

模型还给出盈亏平衡阈值：即使采用乐观激活率，如果月均下单频次低于1.2次，服务也无法覆盖固定运营成本。管理层把这一阈值作为试点护栏，而不是只庆祝没有经济价值的注册量。最终文档还把事实与假设分开显示，并为所有外部输入标注日期，便于商户覆盖变化后快速更新。
## Vocabulary
- **addressable** — 可触达的；available to serve in principle.
- **obtainable** — 可获得的；realistically capturable.
- **sensitivity analysis** — 敏感性分析；testing which assumptions matter most.
- **driver model** — 驱动模型；an estimate built from causal inputs.
- **base case** — 基准情景；the scenario considered most likely.
- **downside case** — 下行情景；a scenario with less favorable assumptions.
- **constraint** — 约束；a limit on what the business can achieve.
- **break-even threshold** — 盈亏平衡阈值；the point where value covers cost.
## Grammar Analysis
- “inputs **most likely to change**”用最高级结构指出关键假设。
- “rather than defended”表达应更新而非辩护。
## Data Analyst Extension
机会模型至少拆TAM/SAM/SOM、unit economics、constraints、range和learning plan，禁止只给单点大数。
## Reading Questions
1. Why was $50m weak?
2. Which inputs mattered most?
3. What was the pilot for?
### Workplace Application
Estimate a new segment with uncertain reach and conversion. Write a 40–80 word plan naming drivers, ranges, and one learning test.
### Answer Key
1. The $50 million estimate assumed every resident was reachable, interested, and equally valuable.
2. Order frequency and courier utilization mattered most.
3. The pilot was designed to learn those high-sensitivity inputs.

### Application Model Response

I will estimate the opportunity from reachable customers, expected activation, monthly purchase frequency, unit margin, and delivery constraints. Each driver will have a source, confidence range, and downside/base/upside assumption. Because reach and conversion are uncertain, I recommend a limited-market pilot that measures both before we commit to the full launch cost.
