## English Original

### Retention Changes Across Cohorts

Fictional budgeting app PineBudget saw monthly retention rise from 34% to 39%. The product team credited a new onboarding flow, but the overall figure mixed customers who joined at different times and had different opportunities to return. It also included a growing share of annual-plan users, who naturally retained longer. The average alone could not show whether onboarding improved behavior.

The analyst built weekly acquisition cohorts. Each row contained users who first activated in the same week, and each column showed the share returning in week one, week two, and later weeks. She fixed activation as “connected an account and created a budget,” excluded test accounts, and allowed a seven-day grace period for delayed events. Recent cohorts were compared only at ages they had actually reached.

The chart revealed two stories. Week-one retention improved from 48% to 56% for cohorts exposed to the new onboarding. However, week-four retention remained near 35%. Annual-plan mix explained about two percentage points of the total monthly increase. Onboarding helped users experience initial value, but it did not solve the later decline.

The team segmented cohorts by plan and acquisition channel. The improvement was strongest for organic monthly users and almost absent for users from a discount campaign. Interviews suggested that campaign messaging promised automated savings that the product did not provide. Product kept the onboarding change, while marketing revised the campaign and the lifecycle team tested a week-two reminder.

Cohort analysis makes time and population visible. Analysts should state the cohort entry event, return event, period length, maturity rule, and exclusions. They should also distinguish changes within cohorts from changes in the mix of cohorts. PineBudget did not report that “retention rose because of onboarding.” It reported that onboarding improved early retention for specific groups, while plan mix and later engagement still mattered. That sentence was longer, but it was more useful and more honest.

## Chinese Translation

PineBudget总留存从34%升到39%，但总体值混合了不同加入时间和套餐结构。分析师按首次激活周建立同期群，只比较已经达到相同生命周期的用户。

新引导使第一周留存从48%升到56%，第四周仍约35%；年付用户占比变化解释了约2个百分点。分群还发现促销渠道没有改善。团队保留引导，同时修改营销承诺并测试第二周提醒。

## Vocabulary

- **acquisition cohort** — 获客同期群；users acquired in the same period.
- **maturity** — 成熟度；how much observation time a cohort has.
- **grace period** — 宽限期；extra time allowed for delayed events.
- **plan mix** — 套餐结构；relative shares of subscription plans.
- **initial value** — 初始价值；the first useful product outcome.
- **organic user** — 自然用户；a user acquired without paid promotion.

## Grammar Analysis

- “users **who first activated in the same week**”限定同期群成员。
- “Recent cohorts were compared only at ages **they had actually reached**”强调成熟度限制。

## Reading Questions

1. Why was overall retention insufficient?
2. What changed at week one and week four?
3. Which group showed little improvement?

### Workplace Application

Overall retention rises four points while the annual-plan share also rises. Write a 40–80 word analysis plan that separates product improvement from mix change.

### Answer Key

1. It mixed cohort ages and plan composition.
2. Week-one retention improved, while week-four retention stayed flat.
3. Discount-campaign users showed little improvement.

### Application Model Response

The four-point increase may partly reflect the larger share of annual-plan users rather than a product improvement. I will compare equally mature acquisition cohorts within each plan and channel, then decompose the change into within-cohort performance and plan-mix effects. We should assign a product cause only after quantifying both contributions.
