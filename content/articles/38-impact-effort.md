## English Original

### Prioritize Recommendations by Impact and Effort

Fictional subscription company MossCloud finished a churn analysis with twelve recommendations. Leaders liked all of them, but no team knew where to begin. The list mixed a one-day email fix with a six-month billing rebuild and gave every idea equal weight.

The analyst created a decision table. Expected impact estimated retained customers and margin. Confidence reflected evidence quality. Effort included engineering, operations, and ongoing maintenance. Risk covered customer harm and reversibility. Dependencies and owners were explicit. Scores did not replace judgment; they made assumptions comparable.

A failed-payment reminder ranked high because 18% of avoidable churn followed payment failure, a small pilot had worked, and implementation required one sprint. A loyalty program had higher theoretical impact but low confidence, large cost, and several dependencies. The team selected the reminder, a pricing-message test, and research on the loyalty concept.

Each recommendation became a testable action with target population, expected change, guardrails, owner, cost range, and review date. The table included a “do not pursue now” section so rejected ideas did not quietly return without new evidence. After six weeks, actual outcomes updated confidence and future ranking.

Impact-effort matrices can create false precision if vague scores are treated as facts. MossCloud showed ranges and reasons beside every rating. It also considered strategic fit and capacity, not only easy wins. Prioritization is choosing what not to do as much as what to do. A good recommendation helps leaders compare value, evidence, cost, risk, and timing in one place.

Capacity remained a real constraint. Selecting three priorities meant explicitly pausing nine others and communicating that choice to their sponsors. Without this step, teams would have started every idea informally, spreading effort so thinly that none produced reliable evidence.

The review also named one executive accountable for resolving conflicts when impact and effort pointed in different directions.

## Chinese Translation
虚构订阅公司MossCloud完成一次流失分析后，提出了十二条建议。管理层认可所有建议，但没有团队知道应该从哪里开始。清单把一天可以完成的邮件修改与需要六个月的计费系统重构放在一起，而且给予每项建议相同权重。

分析师建立了一张决策表。预期影响用可能挽留的客户数量和利润衡量；信心反映证据质量；投入同时包括工程、运营和持续维护；风险涵盖客户伤害和可逆性；依赖关系与负责人也被明确写出。评分并不替代判断，而是让不同假设可以比较。

支付失败提醒获得较高优先级，因为18%的可避免流失发生在付款失败后，一次小规模试点已经有效，而且实现只需要一个开发周期。忠诚度计划的理论影响更高，但信心较低、成本很大，还有多项依赖。团队最终选择支付提醒、价格文案测试，以及对忠诚度概念开展进一步研究。

每条建议都被改写为可测试行动，包含目标人群、预期变化、护栏、负责人、成本区间和复盘日期。决策表还设置“暂不推进”区域，防止被拒绝的想法在没有新证据时重新出现。六周后，团队用实际结果更新信心，并用于下一轮排序。

如果把模糊评分当成事实，影响—投入矩阵会制造虚假精确。MossCloud在每项评分旁展示范围和理由，也会考虑战略匹配与团队容量，而不是只选择容易完成的事项。确定优先级既是决定做什么，也是明确不做什么。一份好的建议应让管理层在同一处比较价值、证据、成本、风险和时间。

团队容量仍然是真实约束。选择三项优先工作意味着明确暂停另外九项，并向提案人解释选择。如果缺少这一步，各团队可能私下启动所有想法，导致精力被摊薄，没有一项能产生可靠证据。团队还指定一名管理层负责人，在影响和投入指向不同方向时作出最终取舍。
## Vocabulary
- **dependency** — 依赖；work required from another system or team.
- **reversibility** — 可逆性；ease of undoing an action.
- **theoretical impact** — 理论影响；potential value not yet validated.
- **false precision** — 虚假精确；numbers appearing more certain than evidence.
- **confidence** — 信心；strength of evidence behind an expected impact.
- **capacity** — 团队容量；the amount of work a team can realistically take on.
- **review date** — 复盘日期；when results and assumptions will be reconsidered.
- **strategic fit** — 战略匹配；alignment with current company priorities.

## Reading Questions
1. Why did the reminder rank high?
2. What happened to the loyalty idea?
3. Why can a matrix mislead?
### Workplace Application
Compare a quick medium-impact fix with a costly high-impact idea. Write a 40–80 word prioritization recommendation including confidence and next evidence.
### Answer Key
1. The reminder combined measurable impact, pilot evidence, and low effort.
2. The loyalty idea moved into a research step.
3. A matrix misleads when vague scores are treated as precise facts.

### Application Model Response

I recommend shipping the medium-impact fix first because it has pilot evidence, low effort, and limited downside. The larger idea may create more value, but its confidence is low and delivery cost is high. Rather than fund the full build, we should run a two-week prototype that measures adoption and unit economics before reprioritizing.
