## English Original

### Clarify the Request Before Building the Report

At fictional manufacturer StonePeak, a director asked for “a dashboard of customer performance by Friday.” An analyst immediately began building charts. On Thursday, she learned that the director needed to decide which contracts to renew, not review general customer activity. Half the work did not support the decision, and the required margin data was missing.

The analyst created a short intake routine. She asked what decision the work would support, who would use it, when the decision was due, which customers and periods were in scope, and how success would be judged. She also asked what action would change if a metric moved. These questions turned a broad request into a testable deliverable.

For the renewal decision, the agreed output was a one-page table of the 30 contracts expiring within 90 days. It included revenue, contribution margin, service incidents, usage trend, renewal risk, and account owner. The director needed weekly refreshes for six weeks, not a permanent real-time dashboard. Finance owned the margin definition, and sales reviewed risk labels.

The analyst sent a written recap with scope, definitions, exclusions, owners, milestones, and an example row. The director corrected one assumption before development began: subsidiary contracts should be grouped under the parent company. A ten-minute clarification prevented a much larger rebuild. When new requests appeared later, the team recorded them as changes and renegotiated time or scope instead of silently expanding the project.

Clarification is not resistance. It is a way to make limited analysis time useful. Stakeholders may not know the exact metric or chart they need, but they usually know the decision, audience, and deadline. Analysts can propose the most direct output once those facts are clear. StonePeak delivered the renewal table on time and retired it after the cycle ended. The best solution was smaller than the original dashboard request because it matched the real job.

## Chinese Translation

StonePeak主管要“周五前做客户表现看板”，分析师到周四才发现真正决策是选择续约合同，已有图表多数无用且缺利润数据。

她建立需求澄清流程：决策、受众、期限、范围、成功标准和指标变化后的动作。最终交付缩小为90天内到期的30份合同表，并书面确认口径、排除、负责人和样例。十分钟澄清避免了大规模返工。

## Vocabulary

- **intake routine** — 需求接收流程；a standard clarification process.
- **in scope** — 在范围内；included in the agreed work.
- **deliverable** — 交付物；the output promised to a stakeholder.
- **milestone** — 里程碑；a planned checkpoint.
- **renegotiate** — 重新协商；change time or scope by agreement.

## Grammar Analysis

- “contracts **expiring within 90 days**”用现在分词短语限定合同。
- “once those facts are clear”表示提出方案的前置条件。

## Data Analyst Extension

需求单最少包含decision、audience、deadline、scope、definitions、action、owner和done criteria。先交付示例行或低保真草图，再开始完整开发。

## Reading Questions

1. What decision was hidden in the request? 2. What did the final output contain? 3. How were later changes handled?

### Workplace Application

A manager asks for “all marketing data by tomorrow.” Write a 40–80 word clarification message covering decision, audience, scope, deadline, and a proposed first output.

### Answer Key

1. Which contracts to renew. 2. Expiring contracts and decision metrics. 3. As scoped changes with time trade-offs. The message should be helpful while refusing an undefined “all data” scope.
